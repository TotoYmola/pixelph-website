A different kind of server

Not a map you spawn on. A place you live in.

Most servers hand you money, a car and a job menu. PixelPH hands you a name nobody recognises. Everything after that has to be earned through other people — which is exactly what makes it worth having.

  • You start smallNo starter fortune, no shortcut packages. Your first week is meant to feel like a first week.
  • People rememberReputation lives with your character. Burn a contact and you will feel it months later.
  • Systems intersectBusiness, law, medicine and the underground run into each other constantly. That friction is the content.
  • Nothing resetsYour home, your identity and your possessions carry over. You return to a life in progress.
Civilian life

Somebody has to run the city.

Mechanics, drivers, clerks, cooks, couriers, nurses, landlords. The unglamorous roles are the ones that give everyone else something to react to — and they are where most long-running PixelPH stories actually start.

  • Work that puts you in front of other players, not alone in a field
  • Housing, vehicles and belongings that stay yours between sessions
  • A grounded economy where prices and wages are meant to matter
Law & order

Process, not a kill count.

Officers are held to procedure: cause, warrants, interviews, paperwork, court. That restraint is the point. A city where the police have to build a case is a city where crime is worth committing carefully.

  • Structured departments with training, ranks and accountability
  • Investigations that run over days rather than minutes
  • Government and legal roles that other players can actually use
Emergency services

The worst night of someone's life.

Medics arrive after the shooting stops, and what happens next decides whether a scene ends in a hospital bed or a funeral. Injuries, treatment and recovery are played out rather than skipped.

  • Injury and recovery that carry real time and real cost
  • Hospital and rescue roleplay treated as a scene, not a respawn
  • Medics protected by rule so they can work inside dangerous scenes
The underground

Nobody hands you the good work.

There is no menu of heists. There are people who might vouch for you, information worth keeping, and a slow climb from errands to things worth real risk. What you find out, you find out in character.

  • Progression through contacts, trust and a track record
  • Information you can keep, share, trade or lose
  • Methods stay inside the city — discovery is part of the game
How reputation works
Business & economy

Open the shop. Keep it open.

Player-run businesses are the connective tissue of the city: somewhere to meet, somewhere to launder, somewhere to get your car fixed at 3am. Running one well is its own long campaign.

  • Ownership, staff, stock and reputation you have to maintain
  • Dealerships, garages, venues and services other players rely on
  • Commerce that gives criminal and civilian stories a place to meet
You are not here to win the city. You are here to become someone in it.
